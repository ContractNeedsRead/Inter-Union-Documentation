# 18 FLRA 831 Summary

## Context
- **Related Articles**: [[Article 17]], [[2.02.31]] [[Federal Labor Relations Authority] [[Labor Law]] [[2.10.02.03 Requests for Information]] [[Grievance Process]] 
- **Tags**: #FLRA #LaborLaw #RFI #Grievance

## Summary
Respondent acknowledges that under ordinary circumstances, bargaining-unit employees have a statutory right to gather evidence, present, and process a grievance on their behalf. However, in this case, the respondent denies Officer Painter this right due to alleged harassment and intimidation of witnesses while gathering evidence. Additionally, there's a discussion on whether this unfair labor practice proceeding is barred by 5 U.S.C. 7116(d).

### Key Points
1. **5 U.S.C. 7116(d) Application**:
   - The grievance procedure was filed after the unfair labor practice charge.
   - The arbitrator addressed the limitation on Officer Painter's ability to secure evidence, which is central to the unfair labor practice.

2. **Congressional Intent and Definitions**:
   - The term "raised" implies the activation or setting in motion of an issue.
   - The discretion lies with the aggrieved party to choose the forum, which shouldn't depend on the sequence of hearings but on the initial filing.

3. **Flagrant Misconduct Defense**:
   - Respondent argues that Officer Painter's conduct was so egregious that it fell outside the scope of protected activity.
   - The evidence does not support a finding of flagrant misconduct sufficient to remove the protections for gathering evidence for a grievance.

### Remedy Suggested
The General Counsel recommends orders to:
- Cease and desist from interfering with grievance filing and processing rights.
- Rescind any memorandum limiting Officer Painter's rights, expunge references from files, and notify him in writing.
- Post notices affirming these rights and actions taken to all employees at the Butner facility for 60 days.

## Conclusion
The FLRA should consider the initial unfair labor practice charge as controlling, not the sequence of filings or hearings. This approach aligns with the intended discretion granted to aggrieved parties under the statute, ensuring that procedural technicalities do not override substantive rights.
