# Work Schedules & Limits

## Why this matters across crafts

The hour limits are the contract's armor: who can be forced past what, when the shield drops in December, and who cannot be disciplined for leaving at the limit. The armor is thickest at NALC (11.5/60 plus no-discipline protection), solid at APWU, thinner at NPMHU, and essentially absent at NRLCA — where the only stated weekly wall is the 56-actual-hour condition on relief-day work.

## Legal layer

- **[[31.01 ELM 432.3 Work Schedules and Overtime Limits]]** — the 12-hour ceiling and 6-hour meal rule that sit above every contract limit.
- **[[34.01 FLSA 207 Maximum Hours]]** — the statutory 40-hour floor and the §7(b)(2) annual-guarantee frame the rural limits ride on.
- **[[21.03 Overtime]]** — the assignment side of these limits.

## Divergences worth noting

1. **NALC alone protects non-OTDL employees with an 11.5/60 wall — and makes leaving at it discipline-proof** ([[NALC §8.2\|§8.2.D]]). No other contract has either feature.
2. **The 60-hour weekly ceiling is not universal:** NALC and APWU write 12/60 for ODL employees; NPMHU writes only 12/day; NRLCA writes none — the rural weekly wall is the 56-hour relief-day condition and the 2,240 guarantee-year cap ([[NPMHU §8.5\|NPMHU §8.5.C]], [[NRLCA §9.2.A.1\|NRLCA §9.2.A.1]]).
3. **NPMHU's non-ODL shield is the thinnest:** 10/6 in emergencies only, with no 4-of-5 protection ([[NPMHU §8.5\|§8.5.F]]).
4. **NRLCA's breach remedy is structural, not paid:** exceeding 2,240 hours voids the §7(b)(2) agreement and the carrier is paid under §7(a) for the whole guarantee period — the only contract where breaking the cap rewrites the entire pay system ([[NRLCA §9.2.A.1\|§9.2.A.1.m]]).
5. **Volunteering beyond 12 pays 2.5× at NALC and nothing extra at NPMHU** ([[NALC §8.5\|NALC §8.5.G]] vs [[NPMHU §8.5\|NPMHU §8.5.C]]).
