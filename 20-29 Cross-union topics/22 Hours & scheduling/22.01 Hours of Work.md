# Hours of Work

## Why this matters across crafts

The work week sounds like the most boring clause in the book until you compare the four: three crafts share the 40/8-within-10 (or 9) formula, and the rural craft declares a six-day week and forbids Sunday work for regulars. Everything downstream — overtime triggers, scheduling rights, guarantee pay — is built on that first difference.

## Legal layer

- **[[31.01 ELM 432.3 Work Schedules and Overtime Limits]]** — the 12-hour daily ceiling and the 10-consecutive-hour window that sit above every contract's schedule rules.
- **[[21.03 Overtime]]** — the hours article's other half: everything past 8/40 is overtime territory.

## Divergences worth noting

1. **The rural week is six days, and Sunday work is prohibited for regular carriers** — the only craft with a statutory-shape work week rather than the 40/8/10 formula ([[NRLCA §8.1\|NRLCA §8.1]]).
2. **Rural schedules are evaluated, not scheduled:** the workday varies with route evaluation, volume, and conditions — there is no fixed daily schedule to grieve ([[NRLCA §8.2\|NRLCA §8.2]]).
3. **The rural guarantee is a flat 2 hours** for anyone scheduled and reporting — the hourly crafts' 4/8-hour ladder (with the 200-workyear split) has no rural analog ([[NRLCA §8.4\|NRLCA §8.4]]).
4. **APWU alone guarantees PTFs a 24-hour pay-period minimum** ([[APWU §8.8\|APWU §8.8]]).
5. **Wash-up time is computed, not just granted, for rural carriers** — it enters the mini mail survey as miscellaneous time for allergic carriers ([[NRLCA §8.6\|NRLCA §8.6]]).
