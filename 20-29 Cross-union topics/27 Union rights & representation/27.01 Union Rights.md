# Union Rights

## Why this matters across crafts

The institutional rights — solicitation on premises, information from management, access to installations, union leave — are the union's operating license, and the four contracts renew it with different data obligations. All four guarantee non-work-area solicitation, all four give information rights, but the employer's electronic-reporting duties range from a biweekly file (hourly crafts) to NRLCA's six named reports with DACA codes.

## Legal layer

- **[[34.03 NLRA 8(d) 29 USC 158]]** — the §8(d) bargaining-duty anchor beneath the information rights (note: the general NLRA information duty itself is §158(a)(5), flagged in the contracts but not yet transcribed).
- **[[34.02 Postal Reorganization Act Labor Framework]]** — §§1203/1205/1206: recognition, checkoff authority, and the bargaining frame for every right above.

## Divergences worth noting

1. **NRLCA's six named reports are the most concrete data duty in any contract** — the hourly crafts get a biweekly file whose contents live in MOUs ([[NRLCA §31.3\|NRLCA §31.3]]).
2. **Only NRLCA makes the employer find a leave replacement** when convention leave would strand the route ([[NRLCA §24.3\|NRLCA §24.3]]).
3. **NALC's before-choice-schedule convention requests jump the vacation queue** — a priority rule the others don't state ([[NALC §24.2\|NALC §24.2]]).
4. **Meeting cadences split:** APWU and NRLCA locals meet quarterly; NALC's twice a year unless negotiated otherwise ([[APWU §17.5\|APWU §17.5]] vs [[NALC §17.5\|NALC §17.5]]).
5. **NRLCA routes information requests by organizational level** — a routing ladder the hourly crafts leave to practice ([[NRLCA §31.2\|NRLCA §31.2]]).
