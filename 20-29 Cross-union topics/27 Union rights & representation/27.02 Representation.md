# Representation

## Why this matters across crafts

The steward system is the union's working backbone, and the contracts fund it differently: the hourly crafts run a per-tour formula ladder (1/2/3/5 stewards scaling with headcount), the rural craft runs a two-steward-per-office cap with its own inventions — non-payroll area stewards, retired stewards continuing, and hour-banking that converts steward time into leave credit. What a steward can do, and who pays for it, is craft-specific machinery.

## Legal layer

- **[[34.02 Postal Reorganization Act Labor Framework]]** — §1205 is the statutory checkoff authority every Art 17.7/17.6 cites (transcribed in the PRA note).
- **[[27.01 Union Rights]]** — the institutional counterpart: access, solicitation, information.
- **[[28.01 Grievance Procedure]]** — the arena the paid steward time funds.

## Divergences worth noting

1. **The rural steward formula is a per-office two-person cap** — no headcount scaling beyond 50; but the rural contract compensates with non-payroll area stewards and retired-steward continuation, which the hourly crafts lack ([[NRLCA §17.2\|NRLCA §17.2]]).
2. **NRLCA banks steward hours into leave credit** — the only contract where steward time converts into personal time off ([[NRLCA §17.4\|NRLCA §17.4]]).
3. **Remittance timing splits:** 14 days after the pay period (hourly crafts) vs month-end (rural) ([[NRLCA §17.6\|NRLCA §17.6]]).
4. **APWU alone writes the telephone and locker-inspection clauses** — the other contracts leave both to practice ([[APWU §17.8\|APWU §17.8]], [[APWU §17.9\|APWU §17.9]]).
5. **NALC pays steward time through Formal Step A — a step deeper than the others' Step 2-bound payment** ([[NALC §17.4\|NALC §17.4]]).
