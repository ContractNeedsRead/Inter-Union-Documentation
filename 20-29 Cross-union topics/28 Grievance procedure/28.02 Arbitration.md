# Arbitration

## Why this matters across crafts

Arbitration is where the grievance ladder ends, and the terminal station is furnished differently in each book: three tracks at NALC, three panels at APWU, dockets at NPMHU, and national/area panels with a 30-day award deadline and loser-pays costs at NRLCA. What you can schedule, how fast you get an award, and who pays for it are contract-determined — and they differ.

## Legal layer

- **[[34.02 Postal Reorganization Act Labor Framework]]** — §1206(b) authorizes grievance procedures "culminating in binding third-party arbitration," and §1207 runs the *interest* arbitration machinery that now awaits the NPMHU's next contract. Two different arbitrations, one statute.
- **[[33.01 Das 2013 — OTDL priority includes penalty overtime]]** · **[[33.02 Mittenthal 1987 — Penalty overtime and holiday scheduling]]** — the in-vault awards showing the review standard (full text + holdings).
- **[[24.01 Discipline]]** — the highest-volume arbitration docket's home article.

## Divergences worth noting

1. **NRLCA is the only loser-pays regime** — a real economic filter on rural arbitrations ([[NRLCA §15.5\|NRLCA §15.5]]).
2. **NRLCA is the only contract that deadlines the award** — 30 days, softened by "if possible" for interpretive cases ([[NRLCA §15.5\|NRLCA §15.5]]).
3. **NALC's three-track processing center** (removal/>14-day, expedited, regular) is its own architecture — APWU's panels and NPMHU's dockets are ordered by appeal, not by case type ([[NALC §15.4\|NALC §15.4]]).
4. **APWU quantifies hearing economy** — six expedited or three regular cases per date — while NALC/NPMHU rely on backup-case rules ([[APWU §15.5\|APWU §15.5]]).
5. **APWU and NALC get weekly data; NPMHU quarterly; NRLCA none** ([[APWU §15.6\|APWU §15.6]], [[NALC §15.5\|NALC §15.5]], [[NPMHU §15.5\|NPMHU §15.5]]).
