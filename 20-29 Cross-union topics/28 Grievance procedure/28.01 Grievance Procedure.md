# Grievance Procedure

## Why this matters across crafts

The definition of a grievance is word-for-word the same in all four books, but the machinery differs where it counts: how a case is filed and consolidated, whether a separate mediation step exists, who pays the arbitrator, how fast an award must issue, and what the union can see in the administration reports. A steward moving between crafts has to relearn the ladder, not the law.

## Legal layer

- **[[34.02 Postal Reorganization Act Labor Framework]]** — §1206(b): the PRA is what authorizes grievance and adverse-action procedures "culminating in binding third-party arbitration." Every cell in the table above operates inside that grant.
- **[[31.06 ELM 436.4 Back Pay]]** — the documents-in-support-of-claim machinery the settlements flow through.
- **[[34.12 Veterans Preference 5 USC 2108]]** — where a preference-eligible's MSPB election takes a discipline case out of this ladder (see [[24.01 Discipline]]).
- **[[28.02 Arbitration]]** — the terminal step of every ladder.

## Divergences worth noting

1. **APWU has the only mediation step.** Between Step 2 and Step 3, either side can force joint mediation by trained representatives — a whole forum the other three crafts do not have ([[APWU §15.3\|APWU §15.3]]).
2. **Arbitrator costs split three ways:** NALC, APWU, and NPMHU share costs equally; **NRLCA makes the loser pay** (or equitable assessment) — a real economic difference in deciding whether to arbitrate ([[NRLCA §15.5\|NRLCA §15.5]]).
3. **Only NRLCA puts a deadline on the award itself** — 30 days, with an "if possible" caveat for interpretive cases ([[NRLCA §15.5\|NRLCA §15.5]]).
4. **The pay-advance for late settlement money exists only at APWU and NPMHU** (60 days → 70% advance); NALC and NRLCA have no stated equivalent.
5. **NALC's two-tier Step A is unique** — the Joint Step A form with the two-witness cap in discharges has no counterpart in the other ladders' Step 1 ([[NALC §15.2\|NALC §15.2]]).
6. **Direct-to-arbitration shortcuts differ:** APWU lets discipline and LMOU cases skip Step 3; NRLCA lets the union president file interpretive disputes at Step 4 directly ([[APWU §15.2\|APWU §15.2]], [[NRLCA §15.4\|NRLCA §15.4]]).
