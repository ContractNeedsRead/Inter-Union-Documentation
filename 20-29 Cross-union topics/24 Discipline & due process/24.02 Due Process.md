# Due Process

## Why this matters across crafts

Due process in these contracts is a staircase of pre-action protections: private discussion before paper, notice before suspension, review and concurrence before imposition, and representation at the table. The staircase has the same design in all four books — but the tread widths differ: who must sign the review, how much notice a suspension carries, and what a preference-eligible employee trades for the statutory route.

## Legal layer

- **[[34.14 MSPB Adverse Actions 5 USC 7513]]** — the statutory adverse-action procedure the §16.9 elections route around (30-day notice, answer, hearing, §7701 appeal).
- **[[34.12 Veterans Preference 5 USC 2108]]** — who counts as a preference eligible for those elections.
- **[[27.02 Representation]]** — the steward-side counterpart of every protection above.

## Divergences worth noting

1. **The MSPB-election consequences split three ways** — Step B waiver and no scheduling delay (NALC); Step 3 waiver with arbitration parked behind the MSPB and no back pay for the wait (APWU/NPMHU); Step 3 waiver with roll-status protection (NRLCA). See [[24.01 Discipline]] divergence 4 for the full split.
2. **Only NPMHU requires the review to be a signed, dated writing** — a hard evidence trail the other crafts' concurrence rules don't demand ([[NPMHU §16.8\|NPMHU §16.8]]).
3. **Only NPMHU gives the 14-day suspension its own 14-day notice + Step-2 stay** — a distinct mid-tier of process ([[NPMHU §16.5\|NPMHU §16.5]]).
4. **NRLCA's one-year letter-of-warning shelf life** is half the other crafts' two years ([[NRLCA §16.8\|NRLCA §16.8]]).
5. **NALC's no-delay rule is the exception:** for a NALC preference eligible, the arbitration clock keeps running while the MSPB appeal pends — everywhere else the arbitration waits ([[NALC §16.9\|NALC §16.9]]).
