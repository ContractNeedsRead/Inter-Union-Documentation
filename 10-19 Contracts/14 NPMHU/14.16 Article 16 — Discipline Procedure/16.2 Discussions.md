# NPMHU §16.2 — Discussions

## Full text

<!-- p.98 -->
**Section 16.2 Discussions**

For minor offenses by an employee, management has a responsibility to discuss such matters with the employee. Discussions of this type shall be held in private between the employee and the supervisor. Such discussions are not considered discipline and are not grievable. Following such discussions, there is no prohibition against the supervisor and/or the employee making a personal notation of the date and subject matter for their own personal record(s). However, no notation or other information pertaining to such discussion shall be included in the employee's personnel folder. While such discussions may not be cited as an element of a prior adverse record in any subsequent disciplinary action against an employee, they may be, where

<!-- p.99 -->
relevant and timely, relied upon to establish that employees have been made aware of their obligations and responsibilities.

## Summary

Requires private supervisor-employee discussions for minor offenses; such discussions are not discipline, are not grievable, and may not be noted in the personnel folder (personal notations are permitted). They cannot be cited as prior adverse discipline but may, where relevant and timely, show the employee was made aware of obligations.

## Cross-union comparison

The non-disciplinary discussion mechanic is shared across the agreements; the personnel-folder exclusion and the awareness-purpose exception are common features.
