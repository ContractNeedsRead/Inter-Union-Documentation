# APWU §16.1 — Principles

## Full text

<!-- p.100 -->
### Section 1. Principles

In the administration of this Article, a basic principle shall be that discipline should be corrective in nature, rather than punitive. No employee may be disciplined or discharged except for just cause such as, but not limited to, insubordination, pilferage,
<!-- p.101 -->
intoxication (drugs or alcohol), incompetence, failure to perform work as requested, violation of the terms of this Agreement, or failure to observe safety rules and regulations. Any such discipline or discharge shall be subject to the grievance-arbitration procedure provided for in this Agreement, which could result in reinstatement and restitution, including back pay.

## Summary

Discipline is corrective, not punitive, and no employee may be disciplined or discharged except for just cause — insubordination, pilferage, intoxication, incompetence, failure to perform work as requested, agreement violations, or failure to observe safety rules, among others. Discipline and discharge run through the grievance-arbitration procedure, with reinstatement and back pay as possible remedies.

## Cross-union comparison

The corrective-not-punitive principle and the illustrative just-cause list are the postal-standard discipline opening shared with NALC's and NPMHU's discipline articles; the grievance-arbitration remedy language matches across the crafts.
