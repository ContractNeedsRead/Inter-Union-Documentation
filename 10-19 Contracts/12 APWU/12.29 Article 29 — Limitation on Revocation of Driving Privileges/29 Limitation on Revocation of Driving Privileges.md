# APWU §29 — Limitation on Revocation of Driving Privileges

## Full text

<!-- p.128 -->
An employee’s driving privileges, may be revoked or suspended when the on-duty record shows that the employee is an unsafe driver.
Elements of an employee’s on-duty record which may be used to determine whether the employee is an unsafe driver include but are not limited to, traffic law violations, accidents or failure to meet required physical or operation standards.
The report of the Safe Driver Award Committee cannot be used as a basis for revoking or suspending an employee’s driving privileges. When a revocation, suspension, or reissuance of an employee’s driving privileges is under consideration, only the on-duty record will be considered in making a final determination.
An employee’s driving privileges will be automatically revoked or suspended concurrently with any revocation or suspension of state driver’s license and restored upon reinstatement. Every reasonable effort will be made to reassign such employee to non-driving duties in the employee’s craft or in other crafts. In the event such revocation or suspension of the state driver’s license is with the condition that the employee may operate a vehicle for employment purposes, the employee’s driving privileges will not be automatically revoked. When revocation, suspension, or reissuance of an employee’s driving privileges is under consideration based on the on-duty record, such conditional
<!-- p.129 -->
revocation or suspension of the state driver’s license may be considered in making a final determination.
Initial issuance — an employee shall be issued a Certificate of Vehicle Familiarization and Safe Operation when such employee has a valid state driver’s license, passes the driving test of the U.S. Postal Service, and has a satisfactory driving history.
An employee must inform the supervisor immediately of the revocation or suspension of such employee’s state driver’s license.
(See Memo, page 357)

## Summary

Driving privileges may be revoked or suspended only on the on-duty record showing unsafe driving (traffic violations, accidents, or unmet physical/operation standards) — never on the Safe Driver Award Committee's report. A state license revocation or suspension automatically revokes postal driving privileges (restored on reinstatement), with reasonable efforts to reassign the employee to non-driving duties; employment-purpose license conditions avoid automatic revocation and may be weighed in final determinations. Initial issuance requires a valid license, passing the Postal Service driving test, and a satisfactory driving history; employees must immediately report license revocations or suspensions.

## Cross-union comparison

The on-duty-record-only rule and the license-revocation linkage are the postal-standard driving-privileges provisions shared with the other crafts.
