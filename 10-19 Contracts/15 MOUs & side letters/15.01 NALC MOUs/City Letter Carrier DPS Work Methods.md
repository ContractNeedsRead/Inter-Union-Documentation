# Re: City Letter Carrier DPS Work Methods

## Full text

<!-- p.250 -->

Re: City Letter Carrier DPS Work Methods

This Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) represents the parties’ final agreement regarding the October 8, 1998, Joint Work Methods Study to determine the more efficient work method for city delivery routes in delivery units where Delivery Point Sequence (DPS) has been, or will be, implemented. This MOU is based on the results of a joint study conducted by the parties pursuant to Chapter 5 of Building Our Future By Working Together to determine the relative efficiency of the composite bundle and vertical flat casing work methods in a DPS environment. Further, any interim or local agreements for handling the fourth bundle on park and loop and foot routes will continue until conversion to the DPS vertical flat casing work method. In accordance with paragraph 3 of the October 8, 1998, Joint Work Methods Study Agreement the following are the parties’ joint instructions to the field: 1. There continue to be two approved DPS work methods: the composite bundle work method and the vertical flat casing work method. Any other work methods must be approved by Postal Service Headquarters prior to testing or implementation. 2. The parties have analyzed the results of the joint study and have determined that the vertical flat casing work method is the more efficient work method at all sampled percentage levels of DPS. Management may convert those routes that have vertical flat cases and are currently using the composite bundle work method to the vertical flat casing DPS work method.

<!-- p.252 -->

3. On curbline routes and business routes where DPS is planned, but not implemented, management will determine the most efficient DPS work method. All other routes not yet converted to DPS which have vertical flat cases will use the vertical flat casing DPS work method. 4. On those routes where DPS is not currently planned but where DPS is implemented in the future, management will determine the DPS work method. 5. City letter carriers on a park and loop or foot route will not be required to carry more than three bundles. Date: March 21, 2000

## Summary

Final agreement on the October 8, 1998 Joint Work Methods Study: the composite bundle and vertical flat casing methods remain the two approved DPS work methods (others need Headquarters approval); vertical flat casing is preferred where DPS is implemented; interim and local fourth-bundle agreements continue until conversion; carriers on park and loop or foot routes need not carry more than three bundles.
