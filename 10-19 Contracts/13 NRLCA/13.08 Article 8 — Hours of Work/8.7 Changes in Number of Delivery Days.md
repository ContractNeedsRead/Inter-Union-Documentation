# NRLCA §8.7 — Changes in Number of Delivery Days

## Full text

<!-- p.10 -->
### Section 7. Changes in Number of Delivery Days

If, pursuant to present or future law, changes are made in the number of delivery days, the effects of the change on employees will be negotiated by the parties and all provisions of this Agreement inconsistent with the change will be amended to conform with the change.

The Employer shall notify the Union at least ninety (90) days prior to implementation of the contemplated changes in the number of delivery days. If, within ninety (90) days after such notice the parties are unable to agree on the treatment to be given to employees whose wages, hours, and working conditions will be changed, the Union shall be entitled to refer the matter to arbitration within ten (10) days. The arbitrator shall determine whether the Employer’s proposed treatment of such employees is fair, reasonable, and equitable.

The above is not applicable to changes such as those resulting from route evaluations, etc., which are made under the present practice in the Postal Service.

## Summary

If the number of delivery days changes by law, the parties must negotiate the effects and amend inconsistent Agreement provisions. The employer must give the union at least 90 days' notice before implementing; if no agreement is reached within 90 days, the union may refer the matter to arbitration within 10 days, and the arbitrator decides whether the employer's proposed treatment is fair, reasonable, and equitable. Routine changes from route evaluations are carved out of this protection.

## Cross-union comparison

A contingency clause aimed at five-day-delivery legislation; the other crafts' agreements address delivery-day changes through their own Article 8 provisions and joint task forces rather than this negotiate/arbitrate model. The 90/90/10-day sequence is rural-specific.
