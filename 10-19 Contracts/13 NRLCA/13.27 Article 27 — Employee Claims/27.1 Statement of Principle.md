# NRLCA §27.1 — Statement of Principle

## Full text

<!-- p.101 -->
### Section 1. Statement of Principle

Subject to a $10 minimum, an employee may file a claim within fourteen (14) days of the date of when the employee has learned or may reasonably have been expected to have learned of the loss or damage and be reimbursed for loss or damage to the employee’s personal property, except for motor vehicles and the contents thereof, taking into consideration depreciation where the loss or damage was suffered in connection with or incident to employment while on duty or while on postal premises. The possession of the property must have been reasonable or proper under the circumstances and the damage or loss must not have been caused in whole or in part by the negligent or wrongful act of the employee. Loss or damage will not be compensated when it resulted from normal wear and tear associated with day-to-day living and working conditions.

The above procedure does not apply to motor vehicles and the contents thereof. For such claims, employees may utilize the procedures of the Federal Tort Claims Act in accordance with Part 25 of the Administrative Support Manual. Tort claims are for damage or loss of property, personal injury, or death caused by the negligent or wrongful act or omission of an employee (other than the claimant) while acting within the scope of employment. The procedure specified therein shall be the exclusive procedure for such claims, which shall not be subject to the grievance-arbitration procedure. A tort claim may be filed on SF 95, which will be made available by the installation head or designee.

## Summary

Employees may claim (within 14 days of knowledge, subject to a $10 minimum) for loss or damage to personal property — except motor vehicles and contents — suffered on duty or on postal premises, with depreciation considered, where possession was reasonable and no employee negligence contributed; ordinary wear and tear is excluded. Vehicle claims go exclusively through the Federal Tort Claims Act under ASM Part 25, on SF 95, outside the grievance-arbitration procedure.

## Cross-union comparison

The personal-property claim window and the FTCA carve-out for vehicles mirror the other agreements' employee-claims articles; SF 95 and ASM Part 25 are the shared postal instruments.
