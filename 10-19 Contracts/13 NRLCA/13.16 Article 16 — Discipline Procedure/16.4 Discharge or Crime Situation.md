# NRLCA §16.4 — Discharge or Crime Situation

## Full text

<!-- p.87 -->
### Section 4. Discharge or Crime Situation

In the case of discharge, any employee shall be entitled to an advance written notice of the charges against the employee and shall remain either on the job or on the clock at the option of the Employer for a period of thirty (30) days. Thereafter, the employee shall remain on the rolls (non-pay status) until disposition of the employee’s case has been had either by settlement with the Union or through exhaustion of the grievance-arbitration procedure.

When there is a reasonable cause to believe an employee is guilty of a crime for which a sentence of imprisonment can be imposed, the advance notice requirement shall not apply and such an employee may be immediately removed from a pay status.

Nothing in Section 3 will preclude time-off suspensions in instances where modification of a discharge is agreed to by the parties as a settlement or a third party determines to reduce the penalty of discharge to a time-off suspension. Such instances of time-off suspension or non-pay, non-duty status do not entitle the employee to a day of reflection.

## Summary

Discharge requires advance written notice of charges; the employee stays on the job or on the clock (employer's option) for 30 days, then on the rolls in non-pay status until settlement or exhaustion of the grievance-arbitration procedure. Reasonable cause to believe an imprisonable crime was committed waives the notice requirement and permits immediate removal from pay status. Settlement-reduced discharges can be time-off suspensions, with no day of reflection.

## Cross-union comparison

The 30-day notice-and-on-the-rolls discharge model is the standard postal device across the agreements; the crime exception tracks the same carve-out the other crafts use.
