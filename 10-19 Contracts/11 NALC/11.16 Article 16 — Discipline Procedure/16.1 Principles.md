# NALC §16.1 — Principles

## Full text

<!-- p.75 -->
### Section 1. Principles

In the administration of this Article, a basic principle shall be that discipline should be corrective in nature, rather than punitive. No employee may be disciplined or discharged except for just cause such as, but not limited to, insubordination, pilferage, intoxication (drugs or alcohol), incompetence, failure to perform work as requested, violation of the terms of this Agreement, or failure to observe safety rules and regulations. Any such discipline or discharge shall be subject to the grievance-arbitration procedure provided for in this Agreement, which could result in reinstatement and restitution, including back pay.

## Summary

Discipline must be corrective, not punitive. No employee may be disciplined or discharged without just cause, with a non-exclusive list of examples (insubordination, pilferage, intoxication, incompetence, failure to perform work, contract violations, safety rule failures). All discipline is subject to the grievance-arbitration procedure, which can yield reinstatement and restitution including back pay.

## Cross-union comparison

The corrective-not-punitive framing with the named just-cause examples and the express reinstatement-and-back-pay remedy is shared postal architecture, but NALC's just-cause list is stated in the discipline article itself with a distinctive breadth. The other crafts carry similar just-cause clauses with their own enumerations.
