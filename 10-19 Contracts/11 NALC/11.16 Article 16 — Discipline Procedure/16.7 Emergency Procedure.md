# NALC §16.7 — Emergency Procedure

## Full text

<!-- p.77 -->
### Section 7. Emergency Procedure

An employee may be immediately placed on an off-duty status (without pay) by the Employer, but remain on the rolls where the allegation involves intoxication (use of drugs or alcohol), pilferage, or failure to observe safety rules and regulations, or in cases where retaining the employee on duty may result in damage to U.S. Postal Service property, loss of mail or funds,
<!-- p.78 -->
or where the employee may be injurious to self or others. The employee shall remain on the rolls (non-pay status) until disposition of the case has been had. If it is proposed to suspend such an employee for more than thirty (30) days or discharge the employee, the emergency action taken under this Section may be made the subject of a separate grievance.

An employee placed in an off-duty status under this Section may utilize their accrued annual leave during this period.

## Summary

The Employer may immediately place an employee in off-duty non-pay status — remaining on the rolls — for intoxication, pilferage, safety rule violations, or where keeping the employee on duty risks property damage, loss of mail or funds, or injury to self or others. The employee stays on the rolls in non-pay status until the case is disposed of. If a suspension over thirty days or a discharge is then proposed, the emergency action itself may be separately grieved. Employees in this status may use accrued annual leave during the period.

## Cross-union comparison

The emergency placement grounds (intoxication, pilferage, safety violations, property/funds/injury risk) and the separate-grievance option for the emergency action are NALC-specific details. The annual-leave utilization right during off-duty status is a distinctive addition not stated in the other crafts' emergency suspension clauses.
