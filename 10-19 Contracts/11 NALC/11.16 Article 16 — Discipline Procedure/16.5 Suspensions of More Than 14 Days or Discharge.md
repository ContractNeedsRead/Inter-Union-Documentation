# NALC §16.5 — Suspensions of More Than 14 Days or Discharge

## Full text

<!-- p.76 -->
### Section 5. Suspensions of More Than 14 Days or Discharge

In the case of suspensions of more than fourteen (14) days, or of discharge, any employee shall, unless otherwise provided herein, be entitled to an advance written notice of the charges against him/her and shall remain either on the job or on the clock at the option of the Employer for a period of thirty (30) days. Thereafter, the employee shall remain on the rolls (non-pay status) until disposition of the case has been had either by settlement with the Union or through exhaustion of the grievance-arbitration procedure. A preference eligible who chooses to appeal a suspension of more than fourteen (14) days or his/her discharge to the Merit Systems Protection Board (MSPB) rather than through the grievance-arbitration procedure shall remain on the rolls (non-pay status) until disposition of the case has been had either by settlement or through exhaustion of his/her MSPB appeal. When there is reasonable cause to believe an employee is guilty of a crime for which a sentence of imprisonment can be imposed, the Employer is not required to give the employee the full thirty (30) days advance written notice in a discharge action, but shall give such lesser number
<!-- p.77 -->
of days advance written notice as under the circumstances is reasonable and can be justified. The employee is immediately removed from a pay status at the end of the notice period.

## Summary

For suspensions over 14 days or discharge, the employee receives advance written notice of the charges and stays on the job or on the clock — at the Employer's option — for thirty days, then remains on the rolls in non-pay status until settlement or exhaustion of the grievance-arbitration procedure. A preference eligible choosing the MSPB route stays on the rolls in non-pay status until settlement or MSPB exhaustion. Where there is reasonable cause to believe the employee is guilty of an imprisonable crime, the Employer may give a lesser, reasonable notice period, and the employee leaves pay status at the end of the notice period.

## Cross-union comparison

The 30-day advance notice with the Employer's option to keep the employee on the job or on the clock, followed by non-pay roll status through case disposition, is the NALC-specific discharge pre-procedure. The other crafts' discipline articles use their own notice structures, and the express MSPB-route roll-status rule is a NALC detail.
