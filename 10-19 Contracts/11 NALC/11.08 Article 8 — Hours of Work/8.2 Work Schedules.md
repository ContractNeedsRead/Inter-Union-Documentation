# NALC §8.2 — Work Schedules

## Full text

<!-- p.18 -->
### Section 2. Work Schedules

- **A.** The employee’s service week shall be a calendar week beginning at 12:01 a.m. Saturday and ending at 12 midnight the following Friday.
- **B.** The employee’s service day is the calendar day on which the majority of work is scheduled. Where the work schedule is distributed evenly over two calendar days, the service day is the calendar day on which such work schedule begins.
- **C.** The employee’s normal work week is five (5) service days, each consisting of eight (8) hours, within ten (10) consecutive hours, except as provided in Section 1 of this Article. As far as practicable the five days shall be consecutive days within the service week.
- **D.** Full time employees who are not on an “Overtime Desired” list or on the Work Assignment list, shall not be required to work beyond eleven and a half (11.5) hours of
<!-- p.19 -->
work in a day or sixty (60) hours of work in a service week, and shall not be subject to disciplinary action for terminating their tour of duty when these limits on hours of work are reached.

## Summary

The service week runs Saturday 12:01 a.m. through Friday midnight, and the service day is the calendar day on which the majority of work is scheduled. The normal work week is five eight-hour service days within ten consecutive hours, with the five days consecutive as far as practicable. Full-time employees not on an Overtime Desired list or the Work Assignment list are protected from being required to work beyond 11.5 hours in a day or 60 in a service week, and cannot be disciplined for stopping work when those limits are reached.

## Cross-union comparison

The Saturday-to-Friday service week is the shared USPS norm across crafts, but the NALC statement is the one that keys a day/weekly hour ceiling (11.5/60) to list status: the protection attaches only to full-timers who are not on the ODL or Work Assignment list. Other crafts' schedule sections state the same calendar frame without a status-conditional hour cap; the rural craft instead defines a six-day basic week with relief days.
