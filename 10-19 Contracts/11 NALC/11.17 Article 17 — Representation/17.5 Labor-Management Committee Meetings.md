# NALC §17.5 — Labor-Management Committee Meetings

## Full text

<!-- p.82 -->
### Section 5. Labor-Management Committee Meetings

- **A.** The Union through its designated agents shall be entitled at the national, area, and local levels, and at such other intermediate levels as may be appropriate, to participate in regularly scheduled Joint Labor-Management Committee meetings for the purpose of discussing, exploring, and considering with management matters of mutual concern; provided neither party shall attempt to change, add to or vary the terms of this Collective Bargaining Agreement. The parties at the local level will meet at least twice per year, unless locally negotiated otherwise.
- **B.** All other national level committees established pursuant to the terms of this Agreement shall function as subcommittees of the national level Labor-Management Committee.
- **C.** Meetings at the national and area (except as to the Christmas operation) levels will not be compensated by the Employer. The Employer will compensate one designated representative from the Union for actual time spent in the meeting at the applicable straight time rate, providing the time spent in such meetings is a part of the employee’s regular scheduled work day.

## Summary

The union participates in regularly scheduled joint Labor-Management Committee meetings at national, area, local, and intermediate levels to discuss mutual concerns, without changing the agreement's terms, with local meetings at least twice a year unless locally negotiated otherwise. All other national committees function as subcommittees of the national Labor-Management Committee. National and area meetings (except the Christmas operation) are uncompensated, with one union representative paid straight time for meeting time within the regular work day.

## Cross-union comparison

The twice-per-year local meeting minimum, the subcommittee relationship rule, and the compensation carve-out for the Christmas operation are NALC-specific committee mechanics. The other crafts' labor-management meeting provisions set their own frequencies and payment rules without these particulars.
