# NALC §29 — Limitation on Revocation of Driving Privileges

## Full text

<!-- p.98 -->
An employee’s driving privileges may be revoked or suspended when the on-duty record shows that the employee is an unsafe driver.

Elements of an employee’s on-duty record which may be used to determine whether the employee is an unsafe driver include but are not limited to, traffic law violations, accidents or failure to meet required physical or operation standards.

The report of the Safe Driver Award Committee cannot be used as a basis for revoking or suspending an employee’s driving privileges. When a revocation, suspension, or reissuance of an employee’s driving privileges is under consideration, only the on-duty record will be considered in making a final determination. An employee’s driving privileges will be automatically revoked or suspended concurrently with any revocation or suspension of State driver’s license and restored upon reinstatement. Every reasonable effort will be made to reassign such employee to non-driving duties
<!-- p.99 -->
in the employee’s craft or in other crafts. In the event such revocation or suspension of the State driver’s license is with the condition that the employee may operate a vehicle for employment purposes, the employee’s driving privileges will not be automatically revoked. When revocation or suspension of an employee’s driving privileges is under consideration based on the on-duty record, such conditional revocation or suspension of the state driver’s license may be considered in making a final determination.

Initial issuance—an employee shall be issued a Certificate of Vehicle Familiarization and Safe Operation when such employee has a valid State driver’s license, passes the driving test of the U.S. Postal Service, and has a satisfactory driving history.

An employee must inform the supervisor immediately of the revocation or suspension of such employee’s State driver’s license.

[see Memo, page 219]

## Summary

Driving privileges can be revoked or suspended only when the on-duty record shows an unsafe driver — traffic violations, accidents, or failed physical or operational standards — and the Safe Driver Award Committee report may never be the basis. Privileges track state license revocation automatically, with reinstatement on license restoration, except conditional employment-only licenses, which can be weighed in on-duty-record cases. Reasonable efforts must be made to reassign affected employees to non-driving duties. Initial issuance requires a valid license, passing the USPS driving test, and a satisfactory driving history, and employees must immediately report license revocations or suspensions.

## Cross-union comparison

The on-duty-record-only rule with the Safe Driver Award Committee exclusion and the automatic license-tracked revocation with the conditional-license exception are NALC-specific driving-privilege protections. The other crafts' driving-privilege provisions use their own standards, generally without this record-exclusivity structure.
