# Labor Law Fundamentals (APWU Pre-Convention Workshop)

## Purpose

The APWU 2022 Pre-Convention Workshop "Labor Law Fundamentals" (Murphy Anderson PLLC) is a steward-level primer on the National Labor Relations Act as it applies to the Postal Service: why the Postal Service is under the NLRA, how the NLRB works, what an unfair labor practice charge looks like, the union's duty of fair representation, and how to handle Board communications. It is the union's own training material, summarized here for rank-and-file use alongside the vault's statute notes.

## Content

### Part I — The Postal Service and Federal Labor Law

- Unlike other federal agencies, the Postal Service is covered under the National Labor Relations Act (39 U.S.C. § 410(b)(1)). The Postal Service is also covered by OSHA.
- Like other federal employees, postal workers are covered by parts of the civil service laws, including the prohibition on striking (5 U.S.C. § 7311) and the Hatch Act (5 U.S.C. § 7321 et seq.).
- **Interest arbitration:** if the Union and the Postal Service do not settle a collective bargaining agreement, the Postal Reorganization Act requires a panel of arbitrators to decide the terms of the contract (39 U.S.C. § 1207(c)(1)).
- The PRA forbids requiring postal workers to pay union dues as a condition of employment (39 U.S.C. § 1209(c)), but permits a limited window for quitting the union.

### Part II — The National Labor Relations Board

- The NLRB is the independent federal agency that investigates and enforces the NLRA — the main law governing collective bargaining for the private sector and the Postal Service. Created in 1935, it can certify or decertify a union, investigate charges against an employer or a labor organization, encourage settlement, decide cases in hearings before Administrative Law Judges, and enforce orders.
- The **Board** has five members appointed by the President to 5-year terms with Senate consent (one term expiring each year) and acts as a quasi-judicial body deciding cases on formal records.
- The **General Counsel** (appointed to a 4-year term) is the Board's prosecutor, independent of the Board, responsible for the investigation and prosecution of unfair labor practice cases and supervision of the 26 regional offices (each with a Regional Director, Board agents, field examiners, and/or field attorneys).
- **NLRB process:** Charge → Investigation → Hearing → Board Review → Enforcement.
- **Filing a ULP charge:** forms are at www.nlrb.gov or the Regional Office; the allegation can be one sentence and can be amended; the filer becomes the Charging Party and the Postal Service the Respondent; a position statement may be included. A charge must be filed **within six months** of the facts underlying the allegations.
- **Investigation:** the Regional Director assigns a Board agent; the union's evidence is requested first (have it organized before filing); the agent will ask for affidavits — prepare witness testimony beforehand, tell witnesses to be firm if an affidavit mischaracterizes their testimony, and stay in touch so you have an informal chance to respond to the Postal Service's defenses before the Regional Director decides.
- **Determination:** if the Regional Director finds no merit, the investigator offers two options — withdraw the charge without prejudice (usually best) or take a short-form/long-form dismissal.
- **Deferral:** a region may defer a charge to the arbitration process — *Collyer deferral* (charge may have merit but the issue is covered by the contract and the employer agrees to arbitrate) and *Dubo deferral* (a charge is filed while a grievance on the same issue is already pending).
- **Settlements:** non-Board settlements (union/USPS or charging party/APWU — dismissed but reinstatable on violation), informal settlements (NLRB + party — violation leads to a Complaint and hearing), formal settlements (violation is enforceable without re-arguing the merits).
- **Complaint:** if merit is found and the case does not settle, the Region issues a formal Complaint; the General Counsel prosecutes before an Administrative Law Judge, whose findings the Board affirms or modifies.

### Part III — Employer Violations of the Act

- **Section 8(a)(5)** makes it unlawful for an employer to refuse to bargain collectively with employee representatives. **Section 8(d)** defines collective bargaining (see [[34.03 NLRA 8(d) 29 USC 158]] for the statutory text): the mutual obligation to meet at reasonable times and confer in good faith over wages, hours, and other terms and conditions of employment — without compelling either party to agree or concede.
- **Information requests:** the Postal Service must provide information relevant to the APWU's role as bargaining representative; the relevance standard is low — unit-employee information is presumptively relevant, and information of even probable or potential relevance to the union's duties must be disclosed. Both refusing and unreasonably delaying information are violations. The Postal Service may claim confidentiality and ask for a Non-Disclosure Agreement.
- **NLRB consent orders:** the NLRB enforces its orders in federal court; violations can lead to civil contempt and federal court consent orders requiring the Postal Service to cease and desist from failing to provide or delaying information, with tracking, training, notice-posting, and fine schedules.
- **Section 7** protects the right to self-organization, to form, join, or assist labor organizations, to bargain collectively through representatives of the employees' own choosing, to engage in other concerted activities for mutual aid or protection, and the right to refrain from any or all such activities.

### Part IV — The Duty of Fair Representation

- **Standard** (Vaca v. Sipes, 386 U.S. 171, 190 (1967)): a breach occurs only when a union's conduct toward a bargaining unit member is **arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith**; the union may not arbitrarily ignore a meritorious grievance or process it perfunctorily, but the individual employee has no absolute right to have a grievance taken to arbitration.
- **Arbitrary** — no reason behind the decision; the test is whether the behavior is so far outside a wide range of reasonableness as to be irrational (Air Line Pilots Ass'n v. O'Neill).
- **Discriminatory** — the decision rests on a characteristic of the grievant unrelated to the grievance; the most common accusation is different treatment of non-members. Key question: would you reach the same decision on the same facts for someone else?
- **Bad faith** — improper motives shape the decision: revenge for political opposition, decisions benefiting one's own career, misrepresentations, deceit.
- **Obligation to process grievances:** a union is not obligated to process every grievance; investigate fully, follow the National Agreement, and consider the interests of the bargaining unit as a whole, and the Board and courts will defer to the union's judgment — but document the work. The Board cares more about *how* the decision was reached than *what* the decision was.
- **Prior administration directives** (GC Memos 19-01, 19-05) directed Regions to prosecute unions that lost, misplaced, or forgot grievances unless the union proved an "established, reasonable procedure or system to track grievances," and to prosecute untimely communication with grievants and unresponsiveness to member information requests; the **current administration rescinded** those directives.
- **Union liability:** the General Counsel must also prove the employee "would have prevailed" had the grievance been properly processed; the union is then liable for the increase in damages — the full amount the employee would have received. Consequences include back pay, attorneys' fees, notice postings, harmful publicity, and internal division and distraction.

### Part V — Communications with the Board

- **Responding to a DFR charge:** do not ignore NLRB charges even if frivolous; respond promptly and observe deadlines; ask for extensions when needed; keep a written record of conversations with Board agents. If the charge addresses the National (or both Local and National), contact the APWU Director of Industrial Relations. Charges are often improperly filed against the wrong party — charges against the Local are not charges against the National APWU (Constitution Sec. 6(c): chartered subordinate bodies are fully autonomous).
- Board agents often ask for an affidavit immediately; you are not required to give one unless the Region issues a subpoena. If a charged party fails to respond to evidence requests, the agent may decide based on the charging party's evidence alone, and a complaint may issue; agents may also issue subpoenas.
- **E-filing:** since October 2019 (GC Memo 20-01) all affidavits, correspondence, position statements, and evidence in ULP and representation cases must be submitted through the Agency's e-filing system at www.nlrb.gov.
- **Responding to a complaint:** a Charged Party has **14 days** from service to file an Answer responding to each paragraph, with affirmative defenses; if no Answer is filed, the allegations are admitted and the Board may pursue a **default judgment** (liability established without a hearing).

### Part VI — Suggestions on How to Avoid DFR Charges

1. **Document!** Record the reasoning behind filing, dropping, or settling a grievance — documented reasoning defeats an "arbitrary" claim.
2. **Communicate!** Advise grievants of the reasons for settling or dropping; respond to status inquiries and document those communications.
3. **Don't discriminate!** Treat members and non-members the same; the contract covers everyone in the bargaining unit.
4. **Don't get political!** Avoid political considerations in decisions; favoring officers or disfavoring political opponents can make a prima facie case.
5. **Verify!** Keep correct contact information for grievants.
