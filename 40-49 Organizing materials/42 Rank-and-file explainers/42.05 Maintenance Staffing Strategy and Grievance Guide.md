# Maintenance Staffing: A Strategy and Grievance Guide

## Purpose

The APWU Maintenance Division's strategy guide (National Business Agent Doug Mirowski, Eastern Region) for local union officers fighting to bring maintenance staffing to levels that ensure a safe, clean, and healthy work environment. It explains how staffing surveys become an authorized complement, when to file, and how the five (plus six custodial) staffing grievance categories work — the framework behind the sample grievances in this area.

## Content

### Introduction and source documents (preserved)

The guide's first responsibility: know the number of authorized positions for your installation and make sure the complement is maintained, and be vigilant that the work required by approved staffing surveys is performed. Compile the governing documents for your facility size — the basic and most important are MMO-28-97 (including the BMC Interim Staffing Guidelines), the MS-47 (Housekeeping, Postal Facilities), and Administrative Support Manual Chapter 531.7. Also relevant: ASM 531.13 (record keeping), the Maintenance Series handbooks (MS-1, MS-7, MS-10, MS-24, MS-39, MS-45, MS-47, MS-58, MS-63, MS-65), and the National Agreement. Cite the listed Article 19 references on the Step 2 appeal.

### Background (preserved)

Maintenance staffing is generated through a survey performed pursuant to the current applicable Maintenance Management Order (MMO) and Maintenance Series (MS) handbook. The current MMO controlling survey methodology and actual staffing in mechanized offices is MMO 28-97 (Maintenance Workhour Estimating Guide for All Mechanized Offices), which superseded MMO 21-91 and its supplement MMO 29-91 (predecessors: MMO 30-87, MMO 19-77). Custodial staffing is dictated by the MS-47; non-mechanized offices may have separate MS-1 surveys for building and building-equipment maintenance; Bulk Mail Centers use the Interim BMC Staffing Guidelines plus MS-1 and MS-47, with MMO 28-97 where other mail processing equipment is deployed.

### Authorized complement and the approval process (preserved)

Staffing surveys are compulsory for all USPS facilities per MMO 28-97 and MS-47 — but it is the Area approval that changes a survey from proposed or recommended to **authorized complement**. Local management generally cannot increase or fill positions without Area approval, and the Union's ability to enforce surveys in the grievance procedure is generally limited to authorized complement. The contractual approval path: local survey → Senior Maintenance Official reviews and signs → Plant Manager/Postmaster and the Area's Manager of Maintenance Support sign → authorized complement. In practice the process is circuitous: Area suggests cuts, local management negotiates frequencies back, the package goes to the District Complement Committee (which controls the budget), and only then does the Area's approval follow.

### Before filing (preserved)

Work with local maintenance management when they are genuinely fighting for positions; leave a paper trail and set agreed deadlines in writing for each component of the staffing process. The first goal is an approved staffing package or authorized complement; enter the grievance procedure only when there is no movement toward an authorized complement or a long delay in filling positions after approval. Grievance settlements requiring completion of any stage by a certain date — and the entire process "as expeditiously as possible" — are valuable for both completing the process and any later failure-to-staff grievance. Joint management/Union staffing surveys (with APWU Headquarters Staffing Advocates and USPS Area experts) have been effective. If the approved survey is questionable, send it to APWU Headquarters officers noting discrepancies (equipment, operational tours, work space not included).

### The five overall-staffing grievance categories (preserved)

1. **Understaffing** — below the number of employees an approved survey requires → [[41.05 Understaffing Sample Grievance]].
2. **Failure to complete the staffing package** in a proper manner or timely fashion → [[41.02 Failure to Complete Staffing Package]].
3. **Failure to post or fill a newly vacant position** → [[41.03 Failure to Post Newly Vacant Positions]].
4. **Failure to provide any coverage on an operating tour** → [[41.04 Failure to Provide Maintenance Coverage]].
5. **Bypassing required preventive maintenance** → [[41.01 Bypassing Sample Grievance]].

For monetary hour-for-hour remedies, the Union must be able to show it was patient and exhausted every other possibility; document how long management knew of the need (construction announcements, groundbreaking, opening dates), all staffing correspondence and Area approval letters (often by Labor Distribution Code — 38 Custodial, 36 Mail Processing Equipment, 37 Building Equipment), and argue that internal administrative processes are noncontractual and cannot justify delay. On posting: approval of the survey authorizes the positions and creates vacancies that must be posted within 30 days under [[APWU §38.4]] (38.4.A.2) and filled within 29 more days (38.4.A.1, 38.5.C.3–4); arbitrators have supported 90 days as a reasonable time to fill a vacancy through hiring given the Service's register obligations under EL 311, Section 211.

### Custodial staffing (summarized; full text in source)

The guide's custodial half applies the same framework to MS-47 surveys — summarized on Form 4852 (Custodial Workload Analysis), built from Forms 4869 and 4839 into route sheets 4776, with quarterly inspections on Form 4851 — and adds six custodial grievance categories: custodial understaffing; failure to complete the package; not covering long-term custodial absences; failure to develop or assign route sheets (4776); bypassing area cleaning; and bypassing component cleaning. Key custodial points preserved: the Gamser Award (A8-NA-0375) holds the MS-47 binding on the USPS; the 1760-hour workyear (MS-47 §243.u) already embeds 320 hours for leave and holidays, so hour-for-hour short-term absence coverage is probably not winnable, but area cleaning routes must be performed each tour (MS-47 Ch. 4, §340 priority); the single-person office coverage options are in MMO 28-97 Section 5; and on-point custodial awards include Franklin (B90T-4B-C92018695, AIRS #24795), Blackwell (C87T-1C-C90006204, AIRS #25963), Benn (J90T-4J-C94041806, AIRS #25975), Zobrak (D90T-1D-C94020357, AIRS #23747), Belshaw (C0T-4G-C0014749, AIRS #22892), Amis (C90T-4C-C94099760, AIRS #26546), and Byars (H94T-1H-C96055756, AIRS #27529). The full custodial section and the appendix of source documents are preserved in the source file.
