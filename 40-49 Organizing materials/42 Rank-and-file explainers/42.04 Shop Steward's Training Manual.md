# Shop Steward's Training Manual (APWU, July 2022)

## Purpose

The APWU Shop Steward's Training Manual (Research and Education Department, Revised July 2022) — the union's own guide to steward certification, duties, rights, legal protections, and grievance processing. It is the source the five sample grievances in this area ([[41.01 Bypassing Sample Grievance]] ff.) were drawn from, and the reference for how a steward files, documents, and processes them.

## Content

### Introduction and duties (preserved from the source)

The steward is the backbone of the Union: the daily point of contact between the membership and the Union, enforcing the Collective Bargaining Agreement and Local Memorandum of Understanding, motivating and organizing members, keeping members informed, and referring members to the correct agencies. Once certified, the steward becomes the face and voice of the Union. The steward's duties, as the manual lists them: **Enforcer** (ensure the CBA is implemented), **Liaison** (between Union officers, Management, and membership), **Confidant** (hold grievance-investigation information in strictest confidence), **Leader** (role model and credibility builder), **Educator** (know the CBA, JCIM, and the handbooks and manuals), **Communicator**, **Organizer**, and **Political Activist**.

### Certification (Article 17.2)

APWU shop stewards are appointed or elected under a local's constitution and bylaws; it is the APWU's prerogative, not Management's, to decide who is certified, and Management interference must be challenged immediately. The local president must provide a list of all shop stewards/alternates to the Installation Head under [[APWU §17.2]]: all actively employed local officers serving as stewards "must" be certified and on the list (including the local president); steward lists are posted on Union bulletin boards; stewards wear badges. Stewards filing grievances outside their designated section and/or tour must have a special letter from the local president for each specific issue; small-office and off-payroll certification is covered by the CBA's 17.2.C and D; cross-craft designation under 17.2.E. Improper certification can render a grievance procedurally defective.

### Duty of Fair Representation (preserved)

The Union represents all employees — members and non-members — and may not act in a capricious, perfunctory, arbitrary, or bad faith manner: **Arbitrary** (no explanation for failing to appeal when past practice shows similar appeals), **Capricious** (a reason that is not logical), **Perfunctory** (no proper investigation, little or no effort to determine facts), **Bad Faith** (personal feelings, Union politics, or bias). Employee rights: clear contract terms, and a grievance decided on its own merits. Union rights: no duty to process frivolous grievances, freedom to settle on any reasonable interpretation with consistent treatment, and good-faith merit judgments with reasonable care — using other officers or NBAs to review and concur on withdrawals (especially removals) is encouraged. Settlements from improper motives (hostility, political opposition, racial prejudice) are bad faith regardless of merit.

### Shop steward's rights (Article 17.3, preserved)

Per [[APWU §17.3]] and the JCIM: the right to investigate and adjust grievances; to review documents, files, and records necessary for processing the grievance; to interview the aggrieved employee, supervisors, and witnesses; to represent an employee during Inspection Service or OIG interrogation when requested; reasonable time on the clock for grievance forms, appeals (including Step 3), and the Union's additions and corrections to Step 2 decisions; and to process post-removal grievances that are non-disciplinary and were initiated before separation. **Stewards have superseniority** — no involuntary reassignment from a tour or facility/installation while there is any duty assignment for which the steward is qualified, even if it requires the involuntary reassignment of a more senior employee, while APWU bargaining unit employees remain represented by that steward (the rule does not apply to alternate stewards).

### Weingarten rights (reprinted from "The Legal Rights of Union Stewards," Robert M. Schwartz, c 1999)

During investigatory interviews — when (1) management questions an employee to obtain information and (2) the employee reasonably believes discipline or other adverse consequences may result — the employee has the right to union representation (NLRB v. J. Weingarten, Inc., 1975). Not every discussion qualifies: shop-floor conversations about how to do the job, disciplinary announcements of already-made decisions, locker searches, run-of-the-mill medical exams, and shop meetings without investigatory questions do not trigger the right. The manual's Q&A covers: steward admission to an interview; coercion to waive the right; refusing to attend without a steward (only if management makes clear in advance representation will be denied); polygraph pre-test interviews and tests; sobriety tests; counseling sessions tied to discipline records; telephone interviews; steward out sick (management need not delay if another representative is available); a steward's own interview; requests for a particular representative (must be honored when equally available); questions about others; obstruction of legitimate investigations (not protected); and remedies (a Weingarten violation during an interview that yields a confession does not win reinstatement — the usual remedy is a bulletin-board posting — but discharge for requesting a steward or refusing to answer without one wins reinstatement with back pay). APWU does not encourage lie detector tests; consult an attorney.

### Special immunity (preserved)

Sections 7 and 8(a)(1) of the NLRA protect stewards from discipline based on conduct during grievance meetings — the steward assumes the status of an equal when dealing with Management. Immunity attaches when investigating or adjusting a grievance or investigating a specific problem to determine whether to file; it protects a raised voice or heated argument about the grievance, but not workroom-floor disruption, violence, or threats. Safest course in a potential insubordination situation: leave the meeting, put the supervisor on notice of interference, return to work, and file a grievance and/or unfair labor practice charge. Always use PS Form 7020, PS Form 1260, and/or the Union-representation clock ring, and get both supervisors' authorization when leaving or entering a section. A supervisor may terminate an interview by clear, responsible direct order — the steward may question and argue the order's propriety without backing down, and per Step 4 settlement AC-W-26505/W1458-78A, management must explain any delay in a steward's investigation and say when time will be available.

### What is a grievance (preserved, Article 15.1)

"A grievance is defined as a dispute, difference, disagreement, or complaint between the parties related to wages, hours, and conditions of employment. A grievance shall include, but is not limited to, the complaint of an employee or of the Union which involves the interpretation, application of, or compliance with the provisions of this Agreement or any local Memorandum of Understanding not in conflict with this Agreement." Class action grievances: the Union may designate one steward or official, in writing, to file one class action grievance for all affected employees in an office/facility/installation; all affected employees are covered and individual grievances shall not be filed on the same issue; list each affected employee by name and EIN, and state each employee's remedy individually.

### Proper documentation of grievances (summarized; full text in source)

The manual's central chapter (pages 20–83) gives, for each contract-violation category, the documentation needed, steward tips, and the appropriate remedy: AWOL, Administrative Leave/Act of God, Advance Sick Leave, and — continuing in the source — the full range of leave, discipline, and pay topics through the grievance-documentation catalog. The chapter's golden rules: "A Document Supports an Argument. It Does Not Make an Argument!", and file two separate grievances when an AWOL charge and later discipline arise from the same facts (one contract case, one discipline case). The remaining chapters (per the manual's table of contents) cover PS Form 7020 and 1260, steward's legal rights and special immunity, tools of the shop steward, OIG/Inspector interrogation, refusing to disclose information, processing grievances, grievance-file order, chronology sheets, Step 1–3 time limits and forms, direct appeal to arbitration, establishing a grievance committee, the test for just cause, standards determining past practices, and reducing steward stress.
