# Requests for Information (RFI)

## Purpose

A one-page statement of the steward's right to obtain the documents, files, and records needed to investigate a potential grievance, process a grievance, or defend an employee — and of what to do when the request is refused. It is the legal backbone for the data requests in the [[42.01 Labor Checklist]] workflow and the staffing guide's request-and-provide lists.

## Content

### The note (as the author wrote it)

Article 17, Section 3 requires the Employer to provide for a review of all documents, files, and other records necessary in processing a grievance. Article 31, Section 2 requires that the Employer make available for inspection by the unions all relevant information necessary for collective bargaining or the enforcement, administration, or interpretation of the agreement. Under Section 8a(5) of the National Labor Relations Act, it is an Unfair Labor Practice for the Employer to fail to supply relevant information for the purpose of collective bargaining. Grievance processing is an extension of the collective bargaining process.

Requests for Information are submitted to investigate potential grievances, grievances and to defend employees through the collective bargaining process.

## How to use with the vault

- The three authorities the note cites, in-vault: [[APWU §17.3]] (stewards' right to review documents, files, and records necessary for processing a grievance), [[APWU §31.3]] (management's duty to make relevant information available), and the union-rights framework at [[27.01 Union Rights]].
- Use an RFI before filing the staffing grievances — the documentation lists in [[41.05 Understaffing Sample Grievance]] and [[42.05 Maintenance Staffing Strategy and Grievance Guide]] are ready-made RFI subjects: staffing packages, Area approval letters, eMARS/eCBM bypass data, and overtime reports.
- The NLRA §8(a)(5) unfair-labor-practice backing means a refusal to supply relevant information is independently chargeable while the contract grievance proceeds.
