## Holding

The grievance is granted. Grievant G. Robertson, a Special Delivery Craft regular on the overtime desired list, was improperly denied overtime pay on November 23, 1979, when a part-time flexible City Carrier was assigned 6.35 hours of Special Delivery functions at straight time; he is awarded 6.35 hours' pay at overtime rates. Crossing craft lines is prohibited and the contractual exceptions (including "insufficient work") are not to be invoked unless clearly met; management failed to sustain its burden of showing insufficient work in the Letter Carrier unit.

## Summary

Special Delivery work was assigned across craft lines to a part-time flexible City Carrier while a Special Delivery Craft employee on the ODL was available but not called in. Bloch sustained both the crossing-crafts violation and the overtime-bypass violation, awarding 6.35 hours at overtime rates, with the caution that supervisory judgments need only be reasonable on the facts available at the time — but the exceptions to the no-crossing rule must be clearly established.

## Cross-union comparison

The crossing-crafts premise — craft work goes to the owning craft unless an exception is clearly met — is the shared foundation of every craft-jurisdiction clause; the NALC enforces the same premise through Article 7.1 craft definitions and its own cross-craft awards.
